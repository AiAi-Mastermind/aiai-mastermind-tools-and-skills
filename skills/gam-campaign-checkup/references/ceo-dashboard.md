# CEO Dashboard — Blue Belt Math and Signal Light

Phillip's rule: no single source tells the whole story. Combine all three before acting like the CEO.

## The 3×3 CEO Dashboard Grid

| Source | VOLUME — Am I getting enough? | QUALITY — Am I getting the right kind? | VALUE — Am I making money? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Ads Dashboard** | Impressions; Clicks; Phone calls from ad; Impression share | Search Term report; Ad Strength; Quality Score (8+); Match-type distribution | Avg. CPC; Cost per conversion; Top-of-page rate |
| **Campaign Report (`mysfdomain.com` CSV)** | Page views, filtered; Quote starts; Phone clicks; SMS clicks for context only | Tracking URL filter correct?; destination-page quality; mobile experience | **COST PER QUOTE = Spend / Total Leads — THIS IS THE NUMBER.** |
| **CRM / Tracker** | Total quotes worked; Households written; Policies issued | Marketing source filled?; Multi-line ratio; Geographic match | Cost per acquisition; Premium per household; LTV comparison = ROI |

“Never grade a campaign on clicks and impressions. Spend, leads, and households are the only scoreboard.”

## Lead formula

There are three measurable ways a lead reaches the office:

1. A call directly from the ad, counted after 3+ minutes.
2. A quote start from the carrier website.
3. A phone click from the carrier website.

**Total Leads = Conversions + Quote Starts + Phone Clicks**

Do not include page views or SMS clicks in Total Leads.

## Cost per quote

**CPQ = Total Ad Spend / Total Leads**

Deck example:

- Spend = $1,000
- Total Leads = 18
- `$1,000 / 18 = $56 CPQ`

Sweet spot for most State Farm auto campaigns: **$40 to $80 per quote**.

Phillip's current-market framing: **“$65 is acceptable these days, it used to be a lot cheaper.”**

If Total Leads is zero, do not divide. Report spend and zero measured leads, then diagnose.

## Cost per acquisition

**CPA = Total Ad Spend / Households Closed**

The decisive question: **Is CPA less than or equal to the member's household Lifetime Value?**

### Profitable deck example

- Spend = $1,000
- Households closed = 4
- `$1,000 / 4 = $250 CPA`
- LTV = $1,500
- `$1,500 / $250 = 6x`
- Verdict: Green.

### Upside-down deck example

- Spend = $2,000
- Households closed = 1
- `$2,000 / 1 = $2,000 CPA`
- LTV = $1,400
- The campaign spent more than the household is worth. Investigate.

Use the member's own LTV whenever available. If unknown, the workbook permits **$1,500** as a starting estimate for a typical State Farm household; label it as an estimate.

Phillip's rule of thumb from Geoff's review: about **$300 per acquired household** is “generally a good metric,” but the member's own LTV controls the verdict.

## Signal Light rules

| Light | Exact rule | CEO response |
|---|---|---|
| **Green — Keep Going** | Auto CPQ **$40–$80**; CPA at or below LTV; team converts quotes into households | “Don't touch it. Monitor monthly. Consider scaling.” |
| **Yellow — Wait and Watch** | CPQ **$80–$120**, **or Month 1**, **or trending slightly off**, **or recent changes have not settled** | **“Get 30 days of data.”** |
| **Red — Diagnose and Act** | CPQ consistently **$100+**, CPC way above the supplied market range, or junk terms burn meaningful budget | **“Check search terms first. Then ad quality. Then bids.”** |

The Yellow and Red CPQ bands overlap. Resolve with deck qualifiers: consistency, campaign age, recent changes, CPA, LTV, lead quality, and trend. A single $100 CPQ is not automatically Red.

Workbook nuance, verbatim:

> “A $100 cost per quote is not automatically bad. If your team closes 1 in 3 and your LTV is $2,000, that's $300 CPA, a solid investment.”

Month 1 is Yellow because Google is still learning. “Get 30 days of data” unless an obvious red flag appears, such as no delivery or sustained spam/service-only traffic with no quote activity.

## CPC reframe

CPC alone does not answer “Is it working?” Precision Mode may pay more per click but waste less. Volume Mode may pay less per click but create more noise. Return to total spend, total leads, CPQ, households closed, and CPA versus LTV.

Phillip: “Don't worry about that $5 cost per click alone means nothing.” Be honest when CPC is above the member's supplied regional range; then it is a signal to inspect, not a reason to panic.

## Green-flag opportunity cost

When Green, use Phillip's business-owner logic:

> “I'm going to go find something else to do — if I were to spend time to optimize this further, it might not be worth my while.”

“We cannot argue with the result.” Protect a working machine. The one action may be to leave it alone, improve source tracking, or schedule the next CEO review.
