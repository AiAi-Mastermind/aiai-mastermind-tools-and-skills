# Find Your Numbers — White Belt Navigation

Use this reference to coach a member click by click. Input may be screenshots, typed numbers, or CSV exports. Never guess a missing field.

## Before opening anything

Pick one evaluation period, usually the last 30 days. Write the start and end dates down. Use those exact dates in Google Ads and agency website.

**Pro tip:** “The most common mistake: wrong date range. Always double-check the dates match between Google Ads and your campaign report.”

## Google Ads: spend and conversions

Go to `ads.google.com`.

1. Open **Campaigns**.
2. Select the main campaign being reviewed.
3. Set the date picker to the agreed start and end dates.
4. Find **Cost** or total spend.
5. Find **Conversions**.
6. If Conversions is missing, choose **Columns > Modify Columns**, search for **Conversions**, add it, apply, and bring the column near Cost.

For this method, a conversion is a **call directly from the ad lasting 3+ minutes (180 seconds)**. Confirm the account's conversion definition rather than assuming every item in a generic conversions total is one of these qualified ad calls.

### Spend/conversions screenshot must show

- campaign name;
- exact date range;
- total Cost/spend;
- Conversions column.

A CSV is welcome. Keep campaign rows identifiable.

## Call tracking and forwarding-number nuance

A person who taps the number in the ad may see Google's temporary tracking/forwarding number in caller ID. It rings the office, but if the customer later calls that caller-ID number back, they may not reach the expected office line. The team should give or text the office's real number during the conversation.

Use a dedicated trackable number when attribution matters. A number shared across sources can blur which campaign produced the call.

## Google Ads: Ad Strength

1. Select the campaign.
2. Open **Ads** in the left menu.
3. Open or edit the main responsive search ad.
4. Record the displayed Ad Strength: **Poor, Average, Good, or Excellent**.

The screenshot should show the campaign/ad identity and rating. Do not substitute optimization score; Phillip calls that number misleading.

## Google Ads: Search Terms report

1. Go to **Campaigns** and select the campaign.
2. Open **Insights and Reports > Search Terms**.
3. Set the same date range.
4. Sort by **Clicks** or **Cost** descending.
5. Export, or capture enough screenshots to show top terms and their clicks/cost.

The screenshot/export should show search term text, clicks, cost, conversions if available, date range, and campaign identity.

Search terms are what people actually typed. They are not the keywords the account bids on.

## Google Ads: Auction Insights

1. Select the campaign.
2. Open **Insights and Reports > Auction Insights**.
3. Use the same date range.
4. Sort by **Impression share**.
5. Capture your domain and competing domains.

The screenshot should show impression share, top-of-page rate, position-above rate, and outranking share where available. Record the biggest local agent separately from national aggregators.

## Google Ads: four exclusion lists

1. Open **Tools**.
2. Go to **Shared Library > Exclusion Lists**.
3. Confirm these names exist:
   - `301+ List`
   - `Master Common Negative`
   - `Competitor List`
   - `Negative General`
4. Open each list and verify it is applied to the campaign under review.

A list tied to zero campaigns does nothing. The screenshot must show both list names and campaign application, not merely keywords inside.

## agency website campaign report

Do this **on your personal computer**.

1. Open your agency website campaign dashboard.
2. Select **Campaign** in the left navigation.
3. Open **Statistics** (often the default Stats tab).
4. Set the exact same dates used in Google Ads.
5. Scroll to the bottom right.
6. Select the **orange download button**.
7. Open the downloaded spreadsheet/CSV.
8. Filter to the specific Google Ads campaign tracking URL only.
9. Exclude the main domain and unrelated tracking URLs.
10. Record **Page Views, Quote Starts, and Phone Clicks**.

### Campaign-report screenshot/export must show

- exact date range or a note documenting it;
- tracking URL filter;
- page views;
- quote starts;
- phone clicks.

The report tracks two lead paths Google Ads cannot see: quote starts and website phone clicks. It is mandatory for a verdict.

### SMS-click skepticism

Record SMS clicks for context, but do not include them in Total Leads. Phillip's experience: the number looks promising, yet agents often cannot confirm real customer texts arrived. The formula remains conversions + quote starts + phone clicks.

## CRM or commission tracker

Ask for households closed from Google Ads leads for the period or cohort reviewed. Sales outcomes can lag: a quote this month may close later. Use the member's source tracking, not assumptions.

Useful fields are marketing source = Google Ads, household closed date, policies/premium if tracked, and household LTV. If the team does not track source, that can become the one action.

## Anonymous ad lookup

Use a private/incognito browser so personalization is reduced.

1. Open an incognito/private window.
2. Search a target phrase such as `[brand] auto quote [state]`.
3. Do not repeatedly click the member's ad.
4. Record whether the ad appeared, approximate position, message shown, and competitors.

For another geographic view, use Google's anonymous ad preview available from the Google Ads environment rather than pretending to be physically in that market. This is reconnaissance, not the performance verdict.

## Ad Transparency Center reconnaissance

1. Go to `adstransparency.google.com`.
2. Search the competing advertiser or domain.
3. Review active ads.
4. Record promise, geography language, carrier/brand emphasis, and call to action.

Do not copy outside tactics. This step only informs the workshop competition review.

## Keyword Planner for volume checks

1. In Google Ads, open **Tools > Keyword Planner**.
2. Use the discover/forecast option for the relevant phrase.
3. Set the member's actual geography.
4. Record monthly search volume and bid range shown by Google.
5. Compare city, county, and state only when diagnosing weak delivery.

This is a search-volume and bid-range check. A city with only about 10 searches per month may be too narrow. Bring the finding to the Wednesday Huddle before any structural/location change.
